## What it says (quoted from the page)

- Software, maths, science QA and competitive-programming benchmarks "all have 50-200+ minute horizons
  which are currently doubling every 2-6 months"; the original software work found the horizon "has
  doubled every 7 months".
- Visual computer-use benchmarks (OSWorld, WebArena) have "time horizons ... 40-100x shorter than the
  software and reasoning cluster", with a similar growth rate about two years behind.
- "All domains are consistent with exponential or slightly superexponential growth"; "in no domain we
  examined is progress clearly sub-exponential."
- Caveats from the authors: "Any individual benchmark's result should be treated as fairly noisy";
  many horizons extrapolate beyond the benchmarks' task lengths; benchmarks are likely easier than
  real-world tasks; and "In domains where longer-for-humans tasks are not harder for models, time horizon
  may not be an appropriate metric."
